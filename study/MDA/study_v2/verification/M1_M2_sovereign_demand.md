verdict: M1 = PARTLY SUPPORTED. Government-funded communications constellations outside SpaceX/Amazon/China are real, measurable and rising (about US$1B of awards in 2020, US$3-4B a year in 2023-24, US$5-8B in 2026 year to date, with a further ~US$12-15B in named procurements still unawarded). But (a) the largest single budget, the U.S., went the other way in 2026 (Transport Layer Tranche 3 unfunded, US$2.29B backbone to SpaceX); (b) roughly 70% of the manufacturing value is nationally captive according to the forecaster MDA itself cites; (c) the claim of independence from commercial challengers is only half true, because much sovereign money is routed through commercial operators (Telesat, Eutelsat, SES) whose balance sheets are weak.
verdict: M2 = PARTLY SUPPORTED, WEAK. MDA has real non-Canadian sovereign work, but only as a subsystem supplier (antennas, digital payload), never as prime. Disclosed value to date: one authorisation to proceed of "more than C$100M" (IRIS², not yet a definitive contract, not yet in backlog at 30 Sep 2026). Everything else (U.S. SDA antenna subcontracts, Japan defence satellite payload, BAE MEO antennas) is undisclosed in value. My estimate of cumulative non-Canadian sovereign bookings is C$0.2-0.5B, i.e. well under 10-12% of the C$3.9B backlog. Outside Canada MDA has zero sovereign prime wins and zero AURORA satellite sales to a government programme.
magnitude I would defend: awarded sovereign/military comms satellite work outside SpaceX/Amazon/China of US$3-7B a year (2023-2026), of which the part genuinely open to a Canadian supplier is a subsystem slice of perhaps US$0.3-1B a year. Identified government-funded comms satellites to be built 2025-2034 outside the excluded groups: roughly 2,000-4,000, not the 8,000-15,000 that "20-30% addressable" implies.
what would flip this: M1 to "supported" if Germany awards SATCOMBw 4 (EUR 8-10B) and the UK awards Skynet 6 WSS (>GBP 1.5B) on schedule and the U.S. on-ramps non-SpaceX vendors to the Space Data Network (US$800M line requested). M1 to "refuted" if IRIS² slips again or SATCOMBw 4 is cut back or handed partly to Starlink-type service. M2 to "supported" if MDA converts the IRIS² ATP into a definitive contract and extends it to the 264 Layer-2 satellites, wins AURORA content on Korea's K-LEO or a SATCOMBw 4 subsystem role, or Blue Canyon delivers U.S. prime awards. M2 to "refuted" if the IRIS² ATP is not converted (EU rules restrict non-EU suppliers) and ESCP-P MEO remains the only sovereign prime role.

Conclusion: the objection largely holds for prime contracts. ## 7. Strongest counter-evidence, in order

1. The forecaster MDA cites says 7% of manufacturing value is open and 70% is nationally captive.
2. The U.S., the biggest sovereign buyer, defunded the multi-vendor Transport Layer after US$6.1B of awards and gave the US$2.29B replacement to SpaceX.
